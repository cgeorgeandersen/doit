## The idea

Every piece of work breaks down roughly into three parts: **thinking** (working the problem), **doing** (producing the thing) and **judging** (deciding whether it's right and what happens next). For each one you can keep it, augment it with AI, or outsource it entirely.

| | Thinking | Doing | Judging |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Holdout** | Kept | Kept | Kept |
| **Passenger** | Outsourced | Outsourced | Outsourced |
| **Centaur** | Augmented | Outsourced | Kept |
| **Reverse Centaur** | Outsourced | Kept | Outsourced |

The centaur is the goal: the horse does the hauling, and the human holds the reins. AI takes the menial but necessary work, sharpens your thinking as a sparring partner, and leaves the final decision with you. The reverse centaur is the one to avoid: AI decides what to do, and the person does it by hand.

Two checks keep you on the right side:

- **Could I defend this decision without saying "the AI suggested it"?** If yes, you're steering. If not, the horse is.
- **What am I doing by hand that I'd gladly hand off if someone offered?** That's work for the horse.

## Why it works

- **Judgment is a muscle.** Every call you make yourself is a rep. When AI makes the call and you just approve it, you skip the rep, and the loss compounds: less critical thinking today leaves less to draw on tomorrow.
- **It's backed by evidence.** A 2025 survey of knowledge workers by Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon found that the more people trusted AI with a task, the less critical thinking they reported putting into it.
- **It keeps the speed.** The goal isn't less AI. It's AI in the right places, so you get the productivity without losing your edge.
- **It keeps accountability clear.** When the judgment stays human, there's always someone who can stand behind the work and put their name on it.
