I've had three careers that turned out to be one.

## Journalism and video

I started as a journalist, telling stories in words and on video. The job was to take something complicated and make it clear to people who had other things to do. You learn to find the one sentence that matters, to show rather than tell, and to respect the audience's time. You also learn that clarity and accuracy are the same discipline.

## Analytics

Analytics was the same instinct pointed at numbers: what does this data actually say, and what should we do about it? The work that mattered most was translation, turning an analysis into a decision someone could make on a Monday morning. A dashboard nobody acts on is a very expensive screensaver.

## AI strategy and governance

AI is the most powerful tool I've worked with, the least understood, and the easiest to oversell. My work now is deciding where it fits, which initiatives go first, and how far to trust the result, and building things that make AI understandable and useful for people who don't work in tech.

The frameworks on this site come from that work. The projects are where I test them in public.

## The through-line

Making complex things clear. In a newsroom that meant a story. In analytics it meant a decision. With AI it means a system people can rely on, because they understand what it's good at and where it fails.

That's what I mean by making AI boring.
