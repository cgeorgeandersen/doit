## Hypothesis

Once people can see their own work, most of their stuck points turn out to need a process fix or a simple rule, not AI.

Most people can't say where AI or automation fits in their job, because nobody ever mapped the job. A new tool arrives, the fit isn't clear, and people go on working the old way. The premise of this tool is simple: **you can't automate work you can't see.**

## Method

A free tool that takes about two minutes.

- **You tap through your week:** which of 21 common workflows you run, roughly how many hours each takes, and which step drags.
- **Every step goes through the four questions** of Fix First, AI Last, in order, and gets one label: fix the process, automate it, AI can help, or keep it human. The first "yes" decides, so the cheapest fix always wins. AI is the third question, not the first.
- **You get a one-page blueprint** of your workflows with the stuck points circled in red, an estimate of how your hours split across the four labels, a range of time you could get back, and three next steps: one fix for this week, one tool to try this month, and one conversation to have with your team.
- **AI steps come with a starter prompt** you can paste into any assistant.
- **It ends with a shareable image** and a post you can edit into your own words, because tools tend to stick when a team looks at its work together.
- **Team leads can combine everyone's links** into a team view that shows shared stuck points and one fix to make together.

How the tool keeps itself honest:

- **No sign-up, no database, no stored answers.** Answers are packed into the link itself, so sharing a blueprint means sharing a link, and nothing is kept on a server. The same trick makes the team view possible without accounts.
- **Stated assumptions.** Answer bands become hours (under two hours counts as one, ten or more as twelve), and the time-back range assumes you capture 35% to 65% of the best case, because nobody gets the best case everywhere. The method is written out on the page.
- **Two minutes or it doesn't happen.** Every answer is a tap, not a text box. Nobody maps their work if mapping is work.

## Result

Live at [mapyourworkflow.com](https://mapyourworkflow.com) since September 2026. Answers live only in each person's link, so there's no central dataset, by design.

What building it showed:

- **Vocabulary is the interface.** "Workflow" and "automation" mean little to most people. "Chasing," "waiting" and "redoing" mean a lot.
- **A link can be both the data and the permission.** Packing answers into the link settled privacy and sharing in a single decision.
- **A tool teaches a framework better than a slide does.** People remember the label their own step received.

## What I'd change

- **Find a private way to see the pattern.** Storing nothing was right for trust, but it means the hypothesis gets tested one blueprint at a time. An opt-in, anonymous tally of labels would test it across everyone.
- **Check the time-back range against real hours.** The 35% to 65% capture band is a stated assumption. A few teams tracked for a month would show whether it holds.
