ÆTHER

A real-time wave field, solved on your GPU

Every pixel you see is a cell in a finite-difference solution of the scalar wave equation, stepped forward tens of thousands of times per second in a fragment shader. Nothing here is a canned animation — diffraction, refraction, interference and resonance all emerge from one line of arithmetic applied to a grid.

∂²u/∂t² = c(x,y)² ∇²u  →  ut+1 = 2ut − ut−1 + (cΔt/Δx)² ∇²ut The Laplacian uses the isotropic 9-point stencil (⅔ on edges, ⅙ on corners) so wavefronts stay round instead of inheriting the square grid. Stability needs cΔt/Δx ≤ 1/√2 — the solver keeps you under it.

Refraction is one number. A "glass" cell simply has a smaller local wave speed, c = c₀/n. Slow the wave down on one side of a boundary and Snell's law, lenses, fibre-optic guiding and total internal reflection all appear for free — no ray tracing, no special cases.

The intensity view is a running time-average of u², exactly what a photographic plate or a photodiode records. Fringes that are invisible in the flickering field snap into a stable pattern after a few hundred steps.
